Dear Editors,

Please consider my manuscript, ``Observation-conditioned bounds define experimental decision criteria for bifunctional enzyme systems,'' for publication as a Research Article in \textit{PLOS Computational Biology}. It addresses a practical problem in mechanistic systems biology: how an experiment can support a quantitative decision when distinct molecular explanations reproduce the same observed response. The central contribution is a conservation-aware framework that derives measurable conditions for class-wide prediction guarantees and identifies complementary observations when mechanism discrimination is required.

The study makes three linked contributions. First, it constructs nitrogen-signaling mechanisms that preserve complete stationary target distributions, then derives a concurrent-count condition under which a proposed GlnE concentration meets a prescribed discrepancy tolerance. Second, a general graph enclosure extends the calculation beyond exact enzyme-concentration cancellation. Its application to AceK--isocitrate dehydrogenase retains two-enzyme-bound target complexes, while published wild-type and mutant kinetic summaries supply a separate test of binding ambiguity and independent-activity constraints. Third, a raw-assay benchmark includes nonlinear fitting, parametric null calibration and thousands of independent trials per evaluation setting. Condition-specific signal fits correct tested background and gain distortions; hidden affinity mixtures still undermine a single-class mechanistic interpretation.

The advance is the connection between a declared biochemical model class, an observable gate and an experimental decision. It complements prediction profile likelihood and model-discrimination design by providing sufficient conditions that do not require selecting one microscopic mechanism. The two enzyme systems and reusable graph algorithm support its relevance to the broad systems-biology readership of \textit{PLOS Computational Biology}.

The study is deliberately model-class conditional. The manuscript distinguishes source-summary calibration, independent biochemical checks and proposed measurements; neither worked concentration criterion is presented as already certified in a native assay. Executable analyses, source provenance, complete trial records and independent numerical verification accompany the submission.

If available, I suggest Julio R. Banga (Computational Biology Lab, MBG-CSIC) as Academic Editor because of his expertise in mechanistic systems biology, model identifiability and experimental design.

This manuscript is original, has not been published, and is not under consideration elsewhere. I am the sole author and declare no competing interests. Reused data and software are cited, with provenance and licenses documented.

Thank you for considering the manuscript.
