Dear Editors,

Please consider my manuscript, ``Observation-conditioned bounds enable experimental decisions despite mechanistic equivalence in bacterial nitrogen signaling,'' for publication as a Research Article in \textit{PLOS Computational Biology}. The work addresses a central problem in computational biology: how mechanistic models can guide experiments when distinct molecular explanations reproduce the same biological response. Its central advance is an observation-conditioned guarantee that converts mechanistic non-identifiability into a quantitative concentration decision, together with an end-to-end benchmark showing when complementary binding measurements can and cannot resolve the ambiguity.

Using the \textit{Escherichia coli} GlnD--PII--GlnE--glutamine synthetase cascade, the study makes three linked contributions. It constructs biochemical mechanism families that preserve complete stationary target-state distributions while predicting different enzyme occupancies and kinetics. It then combines protein conservation with a concurrent PII modification-count constraint to tighten the allowed total-GS difference from 0.370 to 0.041, crossing a prespecified 0.05 tolerance and certifying that a fivefold reduction in GlnE concentration keeps the model-class discrepancy below that tolerance. Finally, it follows a complementary binding experiment from prepared doses through ligand-depletion-aware fitting to statistical discrimination: independent concentration standards correct the tested systematic bias, while an unmodeled affinity mixture can still produce false mechanistic attribution despite a convincing fit.

The study moves beyond showing that several models fit: it identifies predictions shared across an equivalent model class, converts an added observation into an assay-level decision, and tests the measurement process needed to separate models within that class. This integration of structural non-identifiability, biochemical network modeling and quantitative experiment design should interest the broad systems-biology readership of \textit{PLOS Computational Biology} and is transferable to other multisite modification cascades governed by bifunctional enzymes.

The study is deliberately model-class conditional and uses published measurements to define a prospective experimental workflow. The manuscript clearly separates class-wide guarantees from claims about native biology and specifies the matched-assay measurements required for prospective validation. Data, executable analyses, independent numerical checks and complete manuscript sources accompany the submission.

If available, I suggest Julio R. Banga (Computational Biology Lab, MBG-CSIC) as Academic Editor because of his expertise in mechanistic systems biology, model identifiability, uncertainty quantification and experimental design.
