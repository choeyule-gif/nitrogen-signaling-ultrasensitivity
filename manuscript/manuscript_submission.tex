\noindent\textbf{Short title:} Equivalence and measurement in nitrogen signaling

\section*{Abstract}

A biochemical response can be precisely measured yet remain mechanistically ambiguous. We establish three linked results for bacterial nitrogen signaling. First, explicit GlnD and GlnE binding--catalysis transformations preserve complete stationary target-state distributions or downstream responses, extending the ambiguity beyond matching a mean titration. Second, in a specified protein-conserving cascade with all PII and GS modification-count states, sequestration bounds the differences between total observations of free-state-equivalent mechanisms. These bounds account for downstream ladder amplification and yield sufficient enzyme-to-target conditions for controlling interpretation error. Third, the surviving ambiguity determines the useful measurement: state-resolved PII distinguishes ladder and population alternatives, whereas target-free GlnD occupancy and normalized GlnE binding curves distinguish the specified regulatory-state families. Independent nuisance variation determines when improved precision is sufficient and when another observable is required. Established response decomposition and stationary elimination provide the mathematical foundation. Productive regulatory complexes, total-input control, and hidden conformations delimit the conclusions: the constructions do not identify an unrestricted native mechanism or validate the complete physiological nitrogen network. Independent paired-paralog data motivate an additional calibrated assay that can infer sequestration without identifying the common GlnD switch. The study connects specified mechanistic alternatives to quantitative interpretation limits and testable observation choices.

\section*{Author summary}

Different molecular mechanisms can give the same protein response, even when every modification state is measured. We use bacterial nitrogen signaling to ask what can still be learned in that situation. We construct indistinguishable mechanisms, calculate how protein binding can separate their experimentally measured total responses, and identify measurements that address the remaining ambiguity. The resulting bounds specify protein concentrations at which a shared free-protein prediction remains an accurate description of the total readout. This matters because experiments commonly measure both free and enzyme-bound protein together, while many theoretical predictions concern only free protein. Binding measurements can distinguish selected mechanisms that target measurements cannot, but their usefulness depends on uncertainty and on which enzyme states are allowed. We show how to choose between improving measurement precision and changing the observable. The proposed experiments are conditional predictions rather than new observations. Together, the results connect the limitations of response curves to concrete ways of testing competing molecular explanations.

\section*{Introduction}

Ultrasensitivity describes a sharp input--output response, but the shape of that response does not uniquely identify its molecular source. Enzyme saturation, interactions among modification sites, reciprocal regulation, and downstream recognition can produce similar titrations \cite{r1,r2}. A useful mechanistic analysis must therefore answer two questions: which response properties follow from the reaction architecture, and which observations distinguish competing explanations of the same measured response?

The \textit{Escherichia coli} nitrogen-signaling cascade provides a concrete setting for both questions. Glutamine regulates the bifunctional enzyme GlnD, which adds and removes UMP groups on trimeric PII. PII modification states regulate GlnE, which adds and removes adenylyl groups on the twelve-subunit glutamine synthetase (GS) complex \cite{r5,r6,r7}. Both layers combine multisite targets with opposing activities of a shared enzyme. Fig 1 summarizes the regulatory directions and state classes.

High free glutamine favors the UR activity of GlnD, increasing unmodified PII; unmodified PII favors GlnE ATase, driving GS toward higher adenylylation. Published measurements report a glutamine response coefficient near two for the GlnD--PII cycle and approximately 4.5--6.5 for the full cascade, depending on PII concentration \cite{r12,r13}. Proposed explanations include allosteric regulation and bifunctionality \cite{r9,r10}, zero-order and multistep effects \cite{r13}, and selective GlnE activation by fully unmodified PII \cite{r12}. Bifunctionality can suppress sensitivity in some architectures but permit it through sequestration or additional enzyme--substrate complexes in others \cite{r40,r46,r66,r67}. Reconstituted-cycle studies also show that concentration robustness depends on strong glutamine inhibition of UTase and on the enzyme construct \cite{r36}. These alternatives make the observation rule and assay conditions central to any quantitative attribution.

We organize the analysis around three results. Explicit regulatory transformations can preserve complete stationary target-state distributions, not just fitted response coefficients. Protein conservation then bounds departures from this equivalence in a specified two-layer cascade and translates them into assay concentration conditions. Finally, complementary measurements distinguish the stated alternatives only when their predictions remain separated after nuisance variation. Exact response decomposition and stationary elimination supply the foundation \cite{r4,r22,r23,r24,r25,r26,r27,r46}; scaling symmetries and design-dependent identifiability have established precedents \cite{r70,r72,r73}. The contribution is the biochemical construction and the connection between its validity conditions and the next informative observation.

\section*{Results}

\subsection*{Complete target distributions can leave enzyme mechanisms unresolved}

The mathematical foundation is a stationary modification ladder. For a distributive target with $n$ modification sites, a shared bifunctional enzyme, and regulatory exchange restricted to free enzyme, elimination of catalytic complexes gives
\[
 p_i=\frac{c_i u^i}{\sum_{j=0}^{n}c_j u^j},\qquad
 \theta=\frac{1}{n}\sum_i i p_i.
\]
Here $u$ is the ratio of opposing catalytic drives and $c_i>0$ describes the effective ladder. This reduction uses established stationary elimination and rational parameterization \cite{r22,r23,r24,r25}. Differentiation gives $d\theta/d\log u=\operatorname{Var}(i)/n$. Consequently, local sensitivity separates into a ladder factor and switch elasticity. Independent identical sites give $c_i=\binom ni$ and $\theta=u/(1+u)$ for every $n$. Neither site count nor enzyme saturation alone steepens that free-target response. S1 Appendix derives the result and its restrictions; S1--S2 Figs retain the concentration and estimator comparisons.

This distinction matters for the twelve digitized GlnD--PII measurements \cite{r12}. The bounded fit has exponent 2.018 but a physical-occupancy midpoint local coefficient of 1.725. Those are different summaries of the same curve, not interchangeable measurements of cooperativity. Independent and interacting ladders fitted with the same four-parameter switch give RMSEs of 0.0352--0.0392 UMP groups per trimer. Exact inverse constructions can also preserve the mean while changing state fractions or introducing a refractory population. Downstream gain calibration can preserve some of those ambiguities. The titration, plateau, shared-parameter and downstream comparisons are retained in S3--S4 Figs, S2 Appendix and the repository extended-analysis archive; they compare stated models, not experimental probabilities of their native correctness.

A stronger ambiguity survives measurement of every target state. GlnD domain experiments motivate separate catalytic and regulatory functions \cite{r18,r17,r51}, without establishing a unique assignment of model states to ACT domains. Let three free enzyme states have weights $w=(1,G/K_1,G^2/(K_1K_2))$ and UT/UR catalytic specificities $a_j,b_j$. The stationary target drive depends on $A/B$, where $A=\sum_j a_jw_j$ and $B=\sum_j b_jw_j$. The transformation
\[
 K_1\mapsto\lambda K_1,\qquad K_2\mapsto K_2/\lambda,
 \qquad (a_1,b_1)\mapsto\lambda(a_1,b_1)
\]
preserves both numerators: the singly liganded weight and its specificity compensate exactly. It therefore preserves every free PII modification-state fraction at every free glutamine concentration, not merely a fitted mean (Fig 2).

A reference independent-ligand model fitted to the twelve coordinates has RMSE 0.0366 UMP groups per trimer and binding scale $K=0.0520$ mM. Its transported UR specificity-fold scenario is stated in A5--A6 and D4; these coefficients are not independently measured microscopic rates. Transformations with $\lambda=1,2,4$ preserve monotonically decreasing UT and increasing UR capacities but predict singly liganded fractions $1/2,1/3,1/5$ at $G=K$. Under rapid binding and unsaturated conversion, their relative relaxation rates are $1,4/3,8/5$. Average ligand occupancy at that input remains one for all three: measuring an average at a symmetry point can miss the very difference revealed by a resolved enzyme state.

The published six-state GlnE topology supplies a downstream counterpart \cite{r71}. With $g=G/K_G$, $p=P/K_P$ and $u=U/K_U$, its binding polynomial is $Z_E=1+g+p+u+pg/\alpha_1+pu/\alpha_2$. The stationary ATase/AR drive is
\[
 v=\frac{k_P}{k_R}\frac{p+\beta g+\beta'pg/\alpha_1}{u}.
\]
The common binding denominator cancels. Thus $\alpha_2$ is silent in this ratio, while common scaling of $\alpha_1$ and $\beta'$ trades binding synergy against catalysis without changing it. GlnE variants coupled to the GlnD family can preserve the GS response without condition-specific recalibration. They predict different PII-binding halfpoints and reduced relaxation rates (S5 Fig). The direct glutamine term $\beta g$ is retained: the cascade response is not attributable to PII alone.

These are constructive applications of binding-polynomial and scaling ideas \cite{r4,r72,r73}, not a claim that stationary invariants or non-identifiability are new. Their specific contribution is an explicit biochemical family that defeats increasingly resolved target measurements. A state-weight change $w_j\mapsto s_jw_j$, compensated by $k_j\mapsto k_j/s_j$, preserves the corresponding catalytic numerator under the stipulated topology. A15 of S1 Appendix compares each result with prior theory. The construction neither exhausts equivalent mechanisms nor proves uniqueness of a native interpretation.

\subsection*{Conservation converts free-state equivalence into bounds on total observations}

Experiments often count enzyme-bound target, whereas the invariant concerns free target. Finite mass-action binding need not destroy the stationary identity: complex balances recover the same free ratio when regulatory exchange is restricted to free enzyme. Total fractions can nevertheless differ. With one target per enzyme complex, two mechanisms sharing the free fraction and conserved totals satisfy
\[
 |\Delta\theta_{\rm total}|\leq\min(1,E_T/T_T).
\]
This is a conservation bound, not a low-occupancy approximation or a Hill-slope bound. Finite binding changes transients; regulatory exchange on catalytic complexes can change the stationary identity itself. S6 Fig separates these effects.

We then retain four PII states, thirteen GS states, three GlnD regulatory states and a refinement of the GlnE classes in a 120-species mass-action cascade. Catalytic complexes conserve PII, GS and both enzymes. Partly modified PII recognition is specified and varied as a model assumption; glutamine and nucleotide pools are buffered. Let $P_T,S_T,D_T,E_T$ denote total PII trimers, GS dodecamers, GlnD and GlnE. Define
\[
 \epsilon_P=(D_T+2E_T)/P_T<1,\qquad
 \epsilon_S=\min(1,E_T/S_T).
\]
One GlnD can bind at most one PII and one GlnE at most two. The total-variation distance between total PII distributions in the equivalent pair is therefore at most $\epsilon_P$, while their free PII distributions coincide.

Downstream, the specified GlnE drive has the form $r(T)=a+b/T$ for free PII pool size $T$, with $a,b\geq0$. The direct glutamine route supplies $b$. Combining the permitted free-pool range with a ladder sensitivity bound gives
\[
 |\Delta\theta_{GS,\rm total}|\leq
 \min\left\{1,\tanh\!\left[\frac{\nu_*}{4}\log\frac{1}{1-\epsilon_P}\right]+\epsilon_S\right\}.
\]
Independent GS sites permit $\nu_*=1$; $\nu_*=12$ covers any positive twelve-site ladder shared by the pair. Without the direct route, $b=0$, free GS fractions coincide and only $\epsilon_S$ remains. These conclusions require the stated recognition and reaction structure (A10--A11).

Figure 3 tests 8,200 paired conditions. Selected numerical differences lie below the conservative bounds; three independent full-reaction integrations agree with constructed stationary states within $3\times10^{-10}\,\mu$M. These checks verify realizations, not global stability. Inverting the bound gives a usable concentration criterion: if $\epsilon_S=0.001$, keeping total-GS interpretation error below 0.01 is guaranteed by $\epsilon_P\leq0.0354$ for independent sites or $\epsilon_P\leq0.00300$ for an unrestricted twelve-site ladder. Exceeding these sufficient limits does not guarantee detectable separation.

The boundary depends on topology and input control. Allowing the previously silent PII--PII-UMP GlnE class to catalyze adenylylation makes its interaction parameter observable; the selected extension reaches a GS fraction separation of 0.154 (Fig 3D). Yet the compensated family retains a drive $a+b/T+cT$ with log-elasticity magnitude at most one, so the general fraction bound survives. Fixing total instead of free glutamine is another intervention: different enzyme occupancies can produce different free inputs, with $0\leq G_T-G\leq2D_T+E_T$ in this network. These examples identify assumptions whose alteration changes the inference; they do not establish an additional native catalytic route.

\subsection*{Complementary measurements address distinct ambiguities}

The next measurement should target what remains unresolved. State-resolved PII distinguishes the stipulated ladder and population alternatives. The adjacent-state contrast
\[
 \frac{p_{i-1}p_{i+1}}{p_i^2}=\frac{c_{i-1}c_{i+1}}{c_i^2}
\]
is the established distributive-modification invariant \cite{r76}; here its input dependence tests a fixed homogeneous ladder. Rare-state ratios are error-sensitive. For four mean-matched candidates, the minimum pairwise state-distribution distance is largest at 0.747 mM on the disclosed grid. A 10 mM measurement separates the independent and refractory scenarios' predicted fully modified fractions (S4 Fig). These are candidate-dependent designs, not experimentally established optima.

Such state measurements cannot separate the GlnD family, which preserves the entire distribution. Instead, a target-free assay resolving singly liganded GlnD gives non-overlapping ranges 0.444--0.500 and 0.167--0.200 for $\lambda=1$ and 4, even when each candidate's binding scale varies independently by a factor of two (Fig 4A). This assay removes target sequestration; the minimum gap is 0.244 and is unaffected by common catalytic speed.

For GlnE, measure PII-binding titrations with PII-UMP absent and normalize each halfpoint by its zero-glutamine value:
\[
 \frac{K_{P,\rm eff}(G)}{K_{P,\rm eff}(0)}
 =\frac{1+G/K_G}{1+G/(\alpha_1K_G)}.
\]
The unknown PII affinity cancels. If enzyme appreciably depletes PII, subtracting half the enzyme total from the total-PII halfpoint restores the free halfpoint. Measurements at zero and two nonzero glutamine concentrations identify $K_G$ and $\alpha_1$ in the ideal binding model except at $\alpha_1=1$, where the ratio is constant (D5).

Nuisance variation can erase separation before noise is considered \cite{r70}. Under the stated effect-size ranges and known Gaussian errors, five independent GlnD occupancy estimates at fraction SD 0.20, or eight GlnE halfpoint estimates per condition at log SD 0.20, give at least 80\% power at one-sided size 0.05. Each halfpoint requires a full titration. These are synthetic precision budgets, not measured assay performance. Overlapping nuisance ranges cannot be separated by replication alone. Hidden active/inactive conformations can also preserve binding as well as target output (A12), so the designs address specified classes rather than unrestricted microscopic identification.

\subsubsection*{A second paralog supplies a conditional sequestration reporter}

Independent in vivo data measure both modified and unmodified GlnB and GlnK peptides \cite{r7}. Their trajectories separate after nitrogen upshift (Fig 5A), but peptide measurements do not resolve intact trimer states. Purified AmtB binds fully deuridylylated GlnK, and ATP and 2-oxoglutarate regulate this interaction \cite{r77}. This supplies a kinetic-model-independent constraint before calibration: if AmtB-bound GlnK subunits are unmodified, their fraction $s_K$ obeys $s_K\leq1-q_K$, where $q_K$ is the total modified-site fraction. For a homotrimer population, $s_K$ is also the bound-trimer fraction and $s_K\leq p_0\leq1-q_K$. The protein-level ceiling requires neither stationarity nor independent sites, but does require calibrated peptide measurements and the stated binding selectivity. At 15 min before upshift, the paired-peptide point fraction is 0.968, giving a point ceiling of 0.032. The working simultaneous intervals for the component means relax it to 0.162; using a separately measured total instead gives 0.668 (Fig 5D). Thus the available data can constrain binding under the observation model, while the choice and precision of normalization materially affect that constraint. These are upper limits, not occupancy estimates. A second target and a controlled calibration can sharpen the generic limit as follows.

Assume stationary independent-site free ladders, an unsequestered GlnB reporter, constant relative catalytic specificity $\rho$, and binding restricted to unmodified GlnK. With free GlnB modified fraction $q_B$, total GlnK modified fraction $q_K$, and bound GlnK trimer fraction $s$,
\[
 F_\rho(q_B)=\frac{\rho q_B}{1-q_B+\rho q_B},\qquad
 s=1-\frac{q_K}{F_\rho(q_B)}.
\]
The common GlnD drive cancels. A control without AmtB binding calibrates $\rho$ from the ratio of modification odds, provided relative specificities are unchanged. A defined reconstitution permits tighter input control than a cellular deletion. Without calibration, the means do not identify $s$: unrestricted $\rho$ leaves only the generic limit $s\leq1-q_K$.

Monotonicity propagates calibration and measurement intervals. In a synthetic example, control fractions $q_B\in[0.48,0.52]$, $q_K\in[0.73,0.77]$ give $\rho\in[2.50,3.63]$. Assay fractions $q_B\in[0.48,0.52]$, $q_K\in[0.35,0.39]$ imply $s\in[0.441,0.561]$. Allowing absolute error 0.10 in the free-pool relation widens this to $[0.347,0.610]$ (Fig 5C). These deterministic intervals are not experimental confidence intervals. Independent stationary-generator solutions verify the reconstruction; 10,000 admissible synthetic cases verify interval containment (A13).

They do not have established simultaneous coverage across the time course and do not include calibration bias. These ceilings require no stationarity; the calibrated reconstruction in B--C does. None of the curves is a measured bound fraction.}
\end{figure}

The odds formula is a special case of a broader calibration strategy. For any positive free ladder, $d m/d\log u=\operatorname{Var}(i)/n>0$; two ladders sharing one scalar drive therefore define an increasing curve $C=m_K\circ m_B^{-1}$. A no-binding titration can calibrate $C$ without independent sites or a fixed odds ratio. Its transfer across assay conditions must still be tested. An independently bounded reporter partition can be propagated into a free-reporter interval rather than assuming all GlnB is free (A14).

More generally, let free GlnK modification lie in $[F_L,F_U]$, bound modification in $[H_L,H_U]$, and measured total modification in $[Q_L,Q_U]$. Conservation gives the sharp bound-protein fraction set
\[
\left\{s\in[0,1]:\ (H_L-F_L)s\leq Q_U-F_L,\quad
(F_U-H_U)s\leq F_U-Q_L\right\}.
\]
For synthetic free fractions $[0.70,0.80]$ and total fractions $[0.35,0.39]$, permitting bound modification up to 0.15 gives $s\in[0.443,0.692]$, compared with $[0.443,0.563]$ if the bound pool is unmodified. Thus imperfect selectivity need not erase all information. These intervals are sharp within their specified rectangular input sets, not physiological estimates. Equal free and bound modification removes the contrast needed to recover $s$; an empty set rejects the joint observation assumptions.

GlnB/GlnK heterotrimers occur in vivo \cite{r78}. The protein-subunit mixture identity survives mixed composition and requires no stationarity, whereas converting to particle counts or transferring a stationary calibration can require composition and equilibration controls. The available time series supplies neither a matched calibration nor direct occupancy validation. Its role in Fig 5 is therefore to expose the normalization and measurement requirements. Native MS and surface plasmon resonance have independently measured AmtB--GlnK binding \cite{r79}, supporting a direct validation endpoint under matched conditions, not validation by transferring a binding constant between different experimental systems.

\section*{Discussion}

Three results connect ambiguity to experiment. Explicit GlnD and GlnE transformations preserve stationary target outputs; protein conservation bounds how total-protein measurements can separate the corresponding free-state predictions; and complementary observations address distinct surviving ambiguities. The paired-paralog construction extends the third result to a concrete biological measurement: a calibrated second target can report sequestration while canceling an unknown common enzyme drive.

The mathematical ingredients have clear precedents. Stationary elimination, binding-polynomial sensitivities, adjacent-state invariants and scaling non-identifiability are established \cite{r4,r22,r23,r24,r26,r27,r76,r72,r73}. Our contribution is their explicit biochemical construction, the cascade observation bound, and a calibrated measurement framework that separates uncertain free-pool response, reporter partition and binding selectivity. The generalized mixture bound broadens the assay beyond the binomial example while retaining experimentally testable calibration requirements. A15 provides a result-by-result comparison. Neither a generic warning about non-identifiability nor the elementary paired-reporter identity alone constitutes a general new theorem.

The bounds offer a practical distinction between interpretation and discrimination. At low sequestration capacity, total output can approximate a shared free-state prediction while conveying little information about which equivalent mechanism generated it. Higher target-state resolution addresses ladder or population ambiguity, but does not resolve regulatory compensation. Binding assays can address that compensation, while paired-paralog controls can constrain a different quantity, sequestration. Precision becomes useful only after nuisance variation and systematic input differences have been controlled.

These conclusions remain conditional on reaction topology, recognition and observation. A productive GlnE state can expose an otherwise silent interaction parameter; hidden conformations can defeat a binding assay; conserved glutamine can differ between mechanisms that match at fixed free input. The selected finite network is not a validation of the complete physiological cascade. ATP/ADP, UTP, metabolism and expression feedback are outside the core stationary analysis. Site-count states omit spatial modification arrangements, which can matter in other multisite systems \cite{r41}. Glutamine:2-oxoglutarate regulation in another species motivates controlling both inputs but does not supply an E. coli parameter constraint \cite{r75}.

Historical coordinates lack replicate-level errors and varied GlnD as well as PII; independent peptide data lack joint covariance and do not establish stationarity. Numerical checks establish identities and selected realizations, not global optimality or native mechanism selection. The immediate experimental test is therefore a controlled assay: calibrate the free-pool response without AmtB binding, bound reporter partition and bound-pool modification, then compare the inferred interval with an independent direct binding measurement under matched inputs. Agreement would test the stated observation model; disagreement would identify a need to revise specificity, dynamics, recognition or sequestration assumptions.

\section*{Materials and methods}

\subsection*{Reaction models and verification}

Core models use positive mass-action rates, distributive catalytic release and explicitly stated regulatory exchange. Complex balances and zero flux across ladder cuts give the stationary distributions; derivatives and conservation yield the response and observation bounds. S1 Appendix supplies complete reaction schemes, proofs, parameter origins and boundary constructions. S2 Appendix supplies data provenance, fitting sensitivity and prospective assay precision; earlier exploratory analyses are preserved in repository documentation. Shared rate-rescaling constructions are not fitted independently to the same data and are not assigned model-selection probabilities.

The closed cascade enumerates 120 species and 300 directed reactions; an additional productive route gives a 156-species model. Stationary solutions reduce to conserved free-pool balances and are checked in the independently enumerated mass-action vector field. Conserved-input calculations add a scalar glutamine balance. Randomized comparisons, alternative recognition weights and independent BDF integrations test specified realizations. Dimensions, tolerances, seeds and parameter ranges are recorded in A10--A12 and S1 Data.

A source copy with generated results removed completed all sixteen regeneration and verification stages in an isolated virtual environment, including the baseline, all mechanistic extensions and original-workbook extraction. All 22 baseline checks, matched-model verification and three extension test groups passed. The integrated entry point additionally checks 2,000 independent paired-reporter generators and 10,000 admissible interval configurations. The generalized calibration extension is checked against 3,000 independent mixture linear programs, 500 reporter-interval linear programs and 1,000 positive ladder pairs. Machine-readable reports distinguish clean regeneration from subsequent checks. They validate computational statements under the model assumptions, not experimental uncertainty or physiological correctness.

\subsection*{Published data and comparison rules}

The first-layer analysis uses twelve author-supplied digitized coordinate pairs, retained with provenance. Bounded Hill and specified ladder fits use identical observations. Corrected AIC includes the residual variance parameter under a working independent Gaussian model; profile calculations follow established practice \cite{r74}. Coordinate perturbations use prespecified input/output scales, not estimated biological errors. Two thousand refits assess coordinate sensitivity and 300 shared perturbations propagate state predictions. Historical downstream summaries receive shared-parameter comparisons without inventing unreported titration coordinates or likelihoods. Numerical and source details are in S2 Appendix and the repository extended-analysis archive.

The independent Gosztolai et al. workbook and model are retained unchanged from \href{https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.4880003}{doi:10.6084/m9.figshare.4880003}. Extraction records source cells, means, SE and sample counts. Paired-peptide fractions divide modified signal by modified plus unmodified signal; they differ from normalization by a separately measured total. First-order SE uses the conservative sum of absolute contributions because covariance is unavailable. It does not establish joint confidence coverage. Raw copy-per-cell values are preserved; the source model corresponds to $0.00161\,\mu$M per copy per cell, and the workbook's inconsistent printed unit is documented.

\subsection*{Prospective observation design}

State-distribution designs maximize a disclosed candidate-dependent minimum distance. Binding designs profile nuisance parameters independently and use specified known Gaussian errors for illustrative precision budgets; each GlnE halfpoint denotes a complete titration. Paired-reporter intervals follow monotonic endpoint propagation and permit an externally bounded free-relation error. Their deterministic coverage requires that all input intervals and the model-error bound hold jointly. D5 and A13--A14 state degeneracies, assumptions, formulas and independent checks.
